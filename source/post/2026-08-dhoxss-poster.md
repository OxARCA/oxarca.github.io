We are presenting Archos at the **Digital Humanities @ Oxford Summer School** on 24 August 2026 at the University of Oxford.

The poster — *Interdisciplinary Uses of AI in the Humanities: Developing an Epistemically
Grounded LLM-System for Archival Exploration* — sets out the problem of the 'archival deluge'. 

In the test run presented in this poster, using the [Newton's Royal Mint papers of 1692–1700](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/mint), we took a single research question about William Chaloner's (1650 – 1699) prosecution. Archos identified 261 primary sources and produced 5 sections and 27 subsections, with
1,688 sentence-level citations across 85,789 words of source-grounded prose. Whole-run compute
came to roughly $12.70.

If you are at the summer school, come and find us. We are especially keen to talk to anyone with
a collection they would like to discover through the Archos system — see [your collection](/page/datasets.html) for what that involves.
